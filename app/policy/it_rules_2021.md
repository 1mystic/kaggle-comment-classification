# IT Rules 2021 - Intermediary Guidelines (Summary)

Paraphrased summary of the due-diligence obligations relevant to content moderation under India's Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, as amended. This is a working summary for grounding moderation explanations, not a legal document - cite the official Gazette notification for authoritative text.

## Rule 3(1)(b) - Prohibited content categories

An intermediary's due-diligence obligations require it to inform users, via terms of service, not to host, display, upload, publish, transmit, store, update, or share information that:

- **(i) Belongs to another person** and to which the user has no right.
- **(ii) Is defamatory, obscene, pornographic, paedophilic, invasive of another's privacy** including bodily privacy, insulting or harassing on the basis of gender, libellous, racially or ethnically objectionable, relating to or encouraging money laundering or gambling, or otherwise inconsistent with or contrary to the laws of India.
- **(iii) Is harmful to a child.**
- **(iv) Infringes any patent, trademark, copyright, or other proprietary rights.**
- **(v) Deceives or misleads the addressee about the origin of the message, or knowingly and intentionally communicates misinformation or information which is patently false or untrue.**
- **(vi) Impersonates another person.**
- **(vii) Threatens the unity, integrity, defence, security, or sovereignty of India, friendly relations with foreign states, or public order, or incites the commission of a cognisable offence, or prevents investigation of any offence, or insults another nation.**
- **(viii) Contains software viruses or malicious code.**
- **(ix) Is patently false and untrue, written or published with the intent to mislead or harass a person, entity, or agency for financial gain or to cause injury to any person.**

This maps most directly onto our platform's **Hate Speech** and **Severe/Violent** classes (identity-based harassment, incitement, and threats to public order or safety), and onto **Offensive** for gender-based insulting or harassing content.

## Rule 3(1)(d) - Takedown on notice

Upon receiving actual knowledge (via a court order or notification by an appropriate government agency) that information is being used to commit an unlawful act relating to the above categories, an intermediary must remove or disable access within **36 hours**.

## Rule 4(8) - Grievance Officer and reasoned explanation

Significant social media intermediaries must publish a **notification of any action taken on non-compliant information, along with reasons**, to the user who created, uploaded, or shared it - including a specific reference to the clause of the terms of service or policy that was violated. This is the specific transparency obligation this platform's explanation layer is built to satisfy: every moderation action here is paired with a policy citation and a human-readable reason, not just a raw label and probability.

## Rule 4(8) - Appeal mechanism

A user affected by an intermediary's decision must be given an adequate and reasonable opportunity to dispute the action and seek reinstatement, including through a Grievance Appellate Committee mechanism. This is the basis for this platform's **appeal path**: any flagged decision can be contested, triggering a re-evaluation with the original policy context attached.

## Rule 3(1)(m) - Periodic reporting

Significant social media intermediaries must publish periodic compliance reports disclosing details of complaints received and action taken, including the number of specific communication links removed or disabled pursuant to proactive monitoring. This is the basis for this platform's **audit trail** - every decision, its policy citation, and its outcome is logged for exactly this kind of reporting.
